Faculty–Student Engagement @ IIIT Hyderabad

Design Thinking Case Study — Improving Classroom Engagement

Design Thinking Process & Insights

A semester-long human-centered design project focused on improving faculty–student interactions at IIIT Hyderabad using the Double Diamond framework.

Problem Space

Faculty play a critical role in shaping academic outcomes, yet their experience is influenced by fragmented communication, limited informal interaction with students, and institutional constraints. These factors indirectly impact classroom engagement and learning effectiveness.

Research Approach

We followed a structured Design Thinking process aligned with the Double Diamond model. Before designing questionnaires, we applied 5 Whys and Jobs-To-Be-Done to ensure meaningful and unbiased research.

  • Stakeholder mapping across different faculty groups
  • In-depth interviews with faculty and teaching assistants
  • Focus on academic goals, non-academic goals, daily routines, and pain points

Key Insights

  • Students value informal interactions with faculty and peers, which positively influence classroom engagement.
  • Student participation and enthusiasm significantly impact teaching satisfaction.
  • Institutional systems and administrative workflows limit feasibility of backend-heavy solutions.

Personas

Based on affinity mapping and synthesis, multiple faculty personas were identified, including research-focused faculty, teaching-focused faculty, and faculty working closely with TAs.

Problem Prioritization

Using POV tables and feasibility analysis, we prioritized problems that were high-impact yet realistically solvable within institutional constraints. Issues related to financial workflows and core IMS systems were deprioritized due to limited access and feasibility.

Final “How Might We” Statement

How might we improve student engagement in class for both students and faculty so that academic interactions become more effective and meaningful?

Solution Concept

The proposed solution focused on strengthening informal faculty–student connections, recognizing that comfort and trust outside the classroom positively influence formal academic engagement.

  • Lightweight platforms for informal academic interaction
  • Structured yet flexible engagement touchpoints
  • Encouraging faculty–student interaction beyond formal lectures

Prototype & Validation

A low-fidelity prototype was developed and shared for feedback. Testing and iteration focused on ease of adoption, alignment with faculty workflows, and perceived impact on student engagement.

Key Learnings

This project strengthened my skills in qualitative research, problem framing, prioritization under constraints, and applying design thinking methods to real institutional contexts.
